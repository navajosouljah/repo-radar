● What makes it different

Text-to-video generation

Describe a scene in natural language and generate a video clip. Supports scene descriptions with action, lighting, and camera direction - "a dog running through a field at sunset, camera tracking left."

Image-to-video animation

Upload a static image and animate it. Control motion direction, intensity, and camera movement. Useful for bringing product photos, illustrations, and concept art to life.

Controllable motion

Fine-grained control over motion direction, speed, and camera movement. Specify "zoom in," "pan left," "orbit around subject" - rather than hoping the model picks a good camera path.

Scene composition

Multi-subject scenes with specified spatial relationships. "A person on the left walking toward a building on the right" generates video with the described layout.

Model weights available

Full model weights are released under Apache-2.0. Fine-tune on your own data, deploy on your own hardware, integrate into your own pipeline.

Batch generation API

Programmatic API for generating videos at scale. Queue jobs, set parameters, and retrieve results - designed for integration into content pipelines, not just interactive use.

Single best feature

Camera control that actually works

The standout is controllable camera movement. Most open-source video models generate random camera motion or static shots. Orca lets you specify zoom, pan, orbit, and tracking shots with predictable results. For any workflow where camera direction matters - product demos, architectural visualization, social media content - this is the capability that makes it usable.

The problem it solves

AI video generation is dominated by closed-source commercial services. Open-source alternatives exist but lack controllability - you describe a scene and hope for the best.

Before

Pay-per-clip pricing. Services like Runway ($12-76/month) and Pika charge per video. Iterating on a single concept - adjusting motion, camera, timing - costs money every attempt.

No model access. Commercial services provide an API, not model weights. You cannot fine-tune, self-host, or integrate the model into custom pipelines.

Uncontrollable motion. Most open-source video models generate random or unpredictable camera movement. "A person walking" might produce a static shot, a zoom, or a wild camera swing.

After

Generate freely. Run on your own GPU. Iterate as many times as needed without per-clip costs. The only expense is your compute.

Full model access. Apache-2.0 licensed weights. Fine-tune on your own dataset, deploy on your infrastructure, embed in your pipeline.

Directed camera. Specify zoom, pan, orbit, and tracking. The video follows your camera direction instead of picking one randomly.

Set it up

Install Orca and start generating videos from text or images.

1

Install and download models

# Clone the repo
git clone https://github.com/stablyai/orca.git
cd orca

# Install dependencies
pip install -e .

# Download model weights (requires ~8GB)
python scripts/download_models.py

Requires Python 3.10+, CUDA 12+, and a GPU with at least 12GB VRAM (RTX 3060 or better). Model weights are hosted on Hugging Face.

2

Generate your first video

# Text-to-video
orca generate \
  --prompt "a whale breaching from calm ocean water, golden hour lighting" \
  --camera "slow zoom out" \
  --duration 4 \
  --output whale.mp4

# Image-to-video
orca animate \
  --image product_photo.jpg \
  --motion "gentle rotation, zoom in" \
  --duration 3 \
  --output product_spin.mp4

Default output is 720p at 24fps. Generation takes 2-5 minutes per 4-second clip on an RTX 4090.

3

Batch generation

# Define a batch in YAML
# batch.yaml:
# - prompt: "ocean waves crashing on rocks"
#   camera: "slow pan right"
#   duration: 5
# - prompt: "city skyline at night, rain"
#   camera: "slow zoom in"
#   duration: 4

orca batch --config batch.yaml --output ./videos/

Batch mode queues jobs and processes them sequentially. Multi-GPU setups can process jobs in parallel.

In the Wild

  • YC W22 backing. Stably AI is a Y Combinator Winter 2022 batch company. The YC backing lends credibility but also raises questions about the long-term open-source commitment as the company matures. Y Combinator
  • andrew.ooo review. An independent developer reviewed Orca's output quality and noted strong results for simple scenes but visible artifacts on complex multi-subject compositions. Camera control was praised as the standout feature. andrew.ooo
  • YouTube demos. Several YouTube channels have published generation comparisons between Orca and commercial services (Runway, Pika, Sora), with Orca generally rated behind on quality but ahead on controllability and cost.
  • Name collision with Microsoft Orca. Note: stablyai/orca is unrelated to Microsoft's Orca language models. The shared name creates confusion in search results and discussions. Verify the repository owner when reading about "Orca."

Watch out for

  • GPU requirements. Minimum 12GB VRAM for inference. Fine-tuning requires 24GB+. Cloud GPU costs for significant generation workloads can approach or exceed commercial service pricing.
  • Quality gap with commercial services. Orca's output quality is notably behind Runway Gen-3, Pika 2.0, and Sora. Expect visible artifacts on complex scenes, inconsistent faces, and occasional motion glitches.
  • 8GB model download. The model weights require a significant download and storage. Factor this into containerized deployments and CI/CD pipelines.
  • Name confusion. The "Orca" name collides with Microsoft's Orca language models. When searching for help, documentation, or discussions, always include "stablyai" to avoid irrelevant results.
