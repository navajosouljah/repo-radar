#7 this weekZagitta/gpu-unlock

gpu-unlock

A tool that removes the speed limit NVIDIA puts on consumer graphics cards for AI work - up to twice as fast.

If you run AI tasks on a GeForce card, NVIDIA caps your math speed at half of what the hardware can do - this removes that cap, and benchmarks show 25% to 100% real-world gains depending on the workload.

How does it work?

Your problem

Your NVIDIA gaming card can do AI math twice as fast as NVIDIA lets it - they slow it down so you'll buy the expensive professional version.

When it kicks in

You install a small program and restart your computer.

You give it

An NVIDIA GeForce RTX 20, 30 or 40 series graphics card running Linux.

It does

It loads before the NVIDIA driver and removes the artificial speed limit on certain math operations.

  1. Installs itself as a boot-time module that runs before the standard NVIDIA driver loads
  2. Sends a reset-and-reconfigure command to each supported GPU, removing the half-rate cap on FP16, BF16 and TF32 tensor math
  3. The standard NVIDIA driver then loads normally and sees full-speed hardware
You get

The same GPU, the same driver, but AI workloads run measurably faster.

  • Faster AI model responsesAn RTX 4090 goes from 165 to 329 TFLOPS on BF16 matrix math - a 100% increase in raw throughput
  • Real-world speed gainsImage generation runs 30% to 55% faster, fine-tuning runs 25% to 50% faster, and time to first response from a local language model drops 25% to 30%

What changes for me?

Without it

  • Your GeForce card runs AI math at half the speed its hardware supports - NVIDIA reserves full speed for their professional cards
  • Buying the full-speed version (an A100 or H100) costs thousands of dollars more
  • You have no way to tell the card to use its full capability

With it

  • The same card now runs AI math at full speed - verified by benchmarks the project publishes for each card model
  • No driver patch or firmware flash needed - the module loads at boot and works with the standard NVIDIA driver
  • Every change is reversible: remove the boot setting and your card goes back to stock behavior

How hard is it to try?

Effort

5 minutesAbout an hourNeeds a developer
  1. Clone the repository and run make install (Debian/Ubuntu: make install-deb, Fedora: make install-rpm, Arch: make install-arch)
  2. Add gpu_unlock.targets=all to your boot command line (the README gives steps for GRUB, systemd-boot and Fedora)
  3. Reboot and verify with journalctl -k -b | grep gpu_unlock - you should see a line for each unlocked GPU

What it takes on your computer

Space on disk
A small kernel module - the source is under 1 MB. Requires kernel headers for your Linux version.
Memory while it runs
Negligible - the module runs once at boot and uses a small DMA buffer per GPU.
Runs on
Linux only. Supported GPUs: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 20 series (experimental), 30 series (stable, 100+ reports), and 40 series (experimental). RTX 50 series is not supported. Requires sudo for installation.

Should I?

Great if you...

  • Anyone running local AI models on an NVIDIA GeForce card under Linux
  • People doing image generation or fine-tuning who want more speed without buying new hardware
  • Tinkerers who want to see what their GPU can actually do

Skip it if...

  • You run Windows (Linux only; a community Windows port exists but is not maintained by this project)
  • You have an RTX 50 series card (not supported yet)
  • You only use your GPU for gaming or non-AI work (gaming and FP32 performance are unchanged)

Instead, you could look at

  • Windows-GPU-UNLOCKA community Windows port linked from the FAQ - experimental and not maintained by the gpu-unlock team

Watch out for

  • RTX 20 and 40 series are experimental. The project calls RTX 30 series 'stable' with 100+ user reports. RTX 20 and 40 series are labeled experimental with less testing.
  • NVIDIA could patch it out. A future driver update could change how the speed limit is applied, breaking the unlock. The module works with the standard driver today but has no guarantee of future compatibility.
  • Linux only. There is no official Windows support. The README warns against Windows installers circulating online and says the project has no cryptocurrency, no website and no installer beyond the source code.
  • Laptops may silently re-lock. Laptops that power down the discrete GPU when idle can reset the unlock. Suspend also resets cards, though the module re-unlocks them on resume.
