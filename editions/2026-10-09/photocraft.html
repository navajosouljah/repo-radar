How does it work?

Your problem

You need to edit photos - crop, adjust colors, combine layers, remove backgrounds - but Photoshop costs $23 a month and the free alternatives feel different.

You want to composite two images: cut out a subject, drop it onto a new background, and adjust the colors so they match.

When it kicks in

You open PhotoCraft on your computer, or an AI agent opens it through its built-in command interface.

Double-click the app, or type a command in Claude Code - PhotoCraft has over 500 commands an agent can call.

You give it

Your photo files: JPEG, PNG, PSD, TIFF, WebP, RAW and more - including Photoshop's own layered files.

Open a PSD file from a designer and keep all the layers, masks and adjustment layers intact.

It does

It gives you a full editing workspace with layers, masks, brushes and adjustments, all running locally.

  1. Pick from 52 tools: selection, brush, pen, shape, text, gradient, eyedropper and more
  2. Stack layers, masks and 16 adjustment layers (Curves, Levels, Hue/Saturation) with 27 blend modes
  3. Apply filters with live previews, or transform and warp with full undo history
  4. Save as PSD, TIFF, PNG, JPEG or WebP - with PSD files round-tripping cleanly (134 of 135 test files preserved)

The README demo: open a multi-layer PSD, adjust the curves, paint on a mask, and save - the file comes back usable in Photoshop.

You get

An edited photo or layered project file, saved on your own computer.

  • Edited photo filesA JPEG or PNG ready to post, or a layered PSD you can hand off to a designer
  • Non-destructive editsEvery adjustment layer and mask is preserved - come back tomorrow and change anything
  • No subscription billThe same workflow Photoshop charges $23/month for, running on your machine for free

What changes for me?

Without it

  • Editing photos with layers, masks and color adjustments means paying Adobe $23 a month - or learning a free tool that works differently.
  • Opening a designer's PSD file means owning Photoshop, or losing layers and adjustments in a converter.

With it

  • The same layers-and-masks workflow, on your own computer, for free - with keyboard shortcuts Photoshop users already know.
  • Open PSD files with layers intact, edit them, and save back without losing anything.

How hard is it to try?

Effort

5 minutesAbout an hourNeeds a developer
  1. Download PhotoCraft for your computer (Mac, Windows or Linux) and install it.
  2. Open a photo and try the basics: crop it, open a Curves adjustment layer (Image > Adjustments > Curves), and paint on a mask to apply the adjustment to only part of the image.
  3. Try opening a Photoshop PSD file if you have one - check that the layers, masks and adjustments come through.

Should I?

Great if you...

  • You know Photoshop's workflow and want a free alternative that works the same way - layers, masks, adjustment layers, the same shortcuts
  • You want to edit photos on your own machine without an account, a subscription or an internet connection

Skip it if...

  • You need a battle-tested editor for paid client work today - this is in early alpha and its creator says it is 'not anywhere close to ready'
  • You rely on Photoshop plugins or advanced features like generative AI fill - those are not built yet

Instead, you could look at

  • GIMPThe established free photo editor. More stable and complete, but a different interface - not a Photoshop look-alike.
  • PhotopeaA free browser-based editor that opens PSD files. No install needed, but it runs in the cloud, not on your machine.
  • KritaA free painting and illustration app. Stronger for digital art and drawing, lighter on photo-editing features.

Is it safe?

PASSED
  • Security advisories: 0GitHub's advisory list, checked Oct 10
  • Searches for CVEs, vulnerabilities, malware and scams10 results read, including the national CVE database: nothing found
  • Are the stars earned?37.5K stars, 162 contributors, 1.1K commits. findarepo: looks organic

Watch out for

  • Early alpha - not ready for professional work. The creator says it is 'not anywhere close to ready.' Users report lag, frozen tools and broken keyboard shortcuts. Expect bugs.
  • Built almost entirely by one person using AI. Brandon Thomas, a developer from Atlanta, built these apps using Claude Opus 5.5 as his AI coding assistant. The repo shows two committers, one with over 550K lines. Long-term maintenance is an open question.
  • 5 of this week's 10 picks are from the same maker. PhotoCraft is part of the ArtCraft suite by storytold. That concentration reflects a real moment - four Adobe replacements launched at once - but it means one org's fate affects half this edition.
  • Well under 50% of Photoshop's depth. All 625 menu items are wired, but many are stubs. Missing: generative AI features, deep typography, about 20 tools, and plug-in compatibility.
