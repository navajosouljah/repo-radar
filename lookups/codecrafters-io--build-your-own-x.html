The problem it solves

Good "build X from scratch" tutorials exist, but they're scattered across the internet with no consistent way to find one that matches your target technology and your language.

Before

Scattered, uneven tutorials. Guides to building a database, a shell, a compiler, or a network stack from scratch exist, but they're spread across random blogs and repos of wildly varying quality.

No filter by stack. Finding one in your specific language, for the specific technology you want to understand, usually means a lot of manual searching.

Black-box tools. Most developers use Redis, Docker, Git, and SQLite daily without ever seeing how they actually work under the hood.

After

One index, hundreds of guides. Tutorials are grouped by target technology (build your own Redis, Git, Docker, shell, etc.) and cross-referenced by programming language.

Pick your stack, go straight to internals. Choose the technology and the language you want to practice in, and jump directly to a vetted starting point.

Learning by building, not reading. The format forces you to implement the real mechanics instead of consuming another explainer article.

Why use it - and what to watch

Zero cost, zero install risk to browse. The honest limits are about content quality and commercial framing, not security.

+ Why it's worth your time

  • Free and instant. No signup, no install - it's a README you read on GitHub.
  • Genuinely comprehensive. Covers databases, shells, compilers, web servers, regex engines, neural networks, and more.
  • Still maintained. Last updated 2026-07-14, not archived, actively taking new submissions via its open issues.
  • The best-known entry point. Reported top-15-ever GitHub star count means it's usually the first result when searching for this format of tutorial.

! Watchouts

  • Quality varies by link. This repo curates but doesn't author most content - individual tutorial quality depends entirely on whoever wrote it.
  • Commercially entangled. Now maintained by CodeCrafters, whose paid platform promotes the same format - expect their product referenced throughout.
  • No LICENSE file. Worth knowing if you intend to reuse or redistribute the list itself, not just read it.
  • No freshness audit per link. The repo doesn't verify each external tutorial is still accurate - check the individual guide's own update date yourself.
